Isombe

Isombe is a Rwandan favorite made from pounded cassava leaves cooked slowly until soft and creamy. Ground peanuts and palm oil give it a rich, earthy taste. It is often served with rice, plantains or boiled potatoes at family meals and celebrations.

Ingredients

  • 500 g pounded cassava leaves
  • 1 leek, ground with the leaves
  • 1 green bell pepper, ground with the leaves
  • 300 g beef, cut into pieces
  • 1 cup beef stock
  • 2 tablespoons palm oil
  • 1 green seasoning cube
  • 3 garlic cloves, freshly ground
  • 3 tablespoons ground groundnuts (peanuts)
  • Salt, to taste

Cooking instructions

Show the steps
  1. Grind the cassava leaves, leek and green bell pepper together into a paste.
  2. Roast the paste in a pan, stirring, until it turns from bright green to a deeper green and loses its bitter taste.
  3. Add the beef and the stock, cover, and let it simmer until the liquid reduces. Stir often so the leaves do not burn at the bottom.
  4. Stir in the palm oil and crumble in the seasoning cube.
  5. Add the ground garlic. Rinse the mortar with a little water and pour it into the pot.
  6. Add a little water and bring it back to a boil, then mix in the ground groundnuts.
  7. Add salt to taste and simmer on low heat until the meat is very tender.
  8. Serve hot with rice or ubugari, a stiff dough made from cassava flour. Isombe tastes even better when reheated the next day.
