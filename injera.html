Injera

Injera is the spongy sourdough flatbread at the center of every Ethiopian meal. It is made from teff, a tiny ancient grain, fermented for a few days until it turns tangy and bubbly. People tear off pieces and use them to scoop up stews, so it works as both plate and spoon.The first batch takes about nine days because of the starter, but you can reuse the starter for later batches.

Ingredients

  • Starter (made once): 2 cups sifted teff flour, 1 teaspoon active dry yeast and 1.5 liters warm water
  • 2 cups teff flour, for the batter
  • 1 teaspoon fenugreek powder (optional)
  • 1.7 liters warm water, about 40 °C (104 °F)

Cooking instructions

Show the steps
  1. Make the starter: mix the teff flour, yeast and warm water, and leave it in a warm spot for 3 days.
  2. Drain off the liquid on top, add about 500 to 600 ml of cold water, and change the water every day for 6 days. Save some of the starter for next time, so you only do this once.
  3. For the batter, blend or mix the teff flour, fenugreek and warm water until smooth.
  4. Cover the batter and leave it overnight.
  5. The next morning, drain off the liquid on top but keep it. Stir most of it back in to adjust the batter to a thin, pourable texture.
  6. Heat a non-stick pan. Pour in the batter, swirl it to cover the base, then cover and cook until holes appear and the top looks dry. Do not flip it.
  7. Slide the injera onto a clean cloth and let it cool before stacking.
